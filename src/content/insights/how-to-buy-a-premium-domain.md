Buying a domain is a financial transaction with a digital asset. The process is simple once the safety rails are in place — and non-negotiable once the price is real.

## 1. Verify the seller actually holds the name

Before any money moves, confirm ownership: the WHOIS record, the nameservers, and the live site should all point back to the same seller. A clean title means no liens, no disputes, and no pending UDRP proceeding.

## 2. Settle through escrow, never wire-to-stranger

**Escrow.com** is the industry standard for domain sales. The buyer deposits funds, the seller transfers the domain, and escrow releases payment only after the transfer confirms. Neither side carries the counterparty risk alone.

Ask for escrow up front. A seller who resists is not a seller you want.

## 3. Transfer to your own registrar

Once escrow clears, the domain pushes or transfers into *your* registrar account. Typical timeline: **0–5 business days** after cleared payment and signed agreement. After that, DNS is yours — point it wherever the brand lives.

## What good looks like on a listing page

- A stated price (or a clear make-offer path)
- Named seller and reachable contact
- Escrow named explicitly before you have to ask
- Delivery and return terms in plain language
